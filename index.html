One partner, hundreds of clinics.

A Swedish AI dental scribe now in about 100 Swedish clinics1 could take Norway, Denmark and Finland one clinic at a time. Or it could go through the organisations that already reach most of those clinics: the record systems they type into, the distributor that supplies them, the chains that own them, and the associations and congresses they trust. This page maps those organisations and shows what each one gets from a partnership.

Three plays worth more than a sales team

The research turned up two corporate groups and one calendar window that together cover all four Nordic markets. Each one is a single conversation that could open hundreds of clinics.

Play 1

The Planmeca group: record systems and distribution, one owner

  • Opus Dental: 3,500+ clinics and 15,000+ users in the Nordics. It runs an API partner program with a test environment, joint pilots and market exposure.3,4
  • AssisDent (Entteri): the private-sector market leader in Finland.6
  • Plandent: the largest dental distributor in Northern Europe, in all four Nordic countries.5 It has distributed another company's dental AI (Pearl) across the Nordics since Dec 2024.8

All three sit inside Planmeca Group.4,6,8 A note that goes straight into the clinic's record system is the integration story. Plandent's Pearl deal shows they already resell AI to clinics.

Play 2

Colosseum Dental Group: one chain, four countries

  • Smile Tandvård (SE): 60+ clinics, calls itself Sweden's largest private chain.9
  • Colosseum Tannlege (NO): 80+ clinics, 1,200+ staff.10
  • Oral Hammaslääkärit (FI): ~70 sites, ~270,000 visits a year, runs on AssisDent. Medical devices are bought centrally with the group's procurement director, and the scribe is a CE-marked device.11
  • Colosseum Tandlægerne (DK).7

A Swedish Smile clinic is a home-market reference. Oral's central buying points to a group-level decision covering 200+ clinics, with no clinic-by-clinic sales.

Play 3

Congress season: the whole profession in five weeks

  • NTF Landsmøte + Nordental, Stavanger: ~100 exhibitors, and "AI in the clinic" is on the programme.21
  • Finnish Dental Convention, Helsinki, 4–6 Nov. It's Finland's biggest dental event.22
  • Riksstämman + Swedental, Gothenburg, 11–13 Nov, ~170 exhibitors.23

Plandent, Opus, chain leaders and association boards all attend. That means partner meetings can be booked now, before the year's budgets close.

A bridge from Sweden that already exists: one of the scribe's current Swedish partners, the distributor Nordenta,2 has a Danish sister company, Dansk Nordenta, in the same Lifco dental group.12 It's a warm introduction into Denmark through a partner that already knows the product.

Why each partner would say yes

A partnership only happens if the other side gains something it wants anyway. This is the pitch I'd bring to each type of partner.

Record systems

"Give your clinics AI documentation without building it."

AI notes land in your journal, so clinics depend on your system more, not less. Opus already invites partners into joint pilots with market exposure.4 A scribe integration is an AI feature for its 3,500+ clinics with no in-house build.

Distributors

"Add recurring software revenue to every consumables visit."

Plandent's reps already resell Pearl's X-ray AI.8 A scribe that writes clinical notes complements X-ray diagnostics rather than competing with it. Assumption Reseller margin or referral fee to be agreed.

Chains

"Hand admin hours back to every dentist, the same way across the group."

In Sweden, 65% of dentists regularly work overtime on admin, often 1–5 hours a week.13 For a chain, that's capacity it doesn't need to hire. The scribe already offers multi-clinic groups a dedicated contact, change management and ongoing support.14

Associations

"A member benefit that answers your members' biggest complaint."

Associations need tangible member value, and admin load is a top concern.13 The offer: member pricing, plus a course session on AI documentation, GDPR and patient-record law. The Swedish self-employed dentists' association TEV is already a partner,2 which gives a reference model for Norway, Denmark and Finland.

The trust layer partners will ask about, already in place: CE-marked Class I medical device under EU MDR, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certified, data processed in the EU, audio deleted within 24 hours, and patient data not used for training.1 Every partner conversation should open with this. It removes the main objection before anyone raises it.

How I'd run it: the first 90 days

Partnerships stall without someone tracking the next step. Here's how the plan above becomes a working pipeline, with owners, stages and what gets reported each week.

Days 1–30

Close the gaps, book the rooms

  • Book partner meetings at Helsinki and Gothenburg (Plandent, Entteri, Oral, Smile).
  • Map Denmark's record systems. It's the one market this research couldn't source.
  • A one-page brief per lane, with the trust layer up front.
  • Ask Nordenta for an introduction to Dansk Nordenta.
Days 31–60

Open the big doors

  • Apply to Opus's partner program for test-environment access.
  • Use a Swedish Smile clinic as the reference for a group-level Colosseum conversation.
  • Propose a member offer and a course session to NTF and Hammaslääkäriliitto.
Days 61–90

First pilot live, measured

  • One partner pilot in a new market, with a clear success metric (admin minutes saved per dentist).
  • A partner dashboard: pipeline stage, clinics reached and pilot results.
  • Go or no-go on a second market, based on the data.

What this research doesn't know yet

Denmark's record systems

Opus lists only Norway and Sweden sites.3 I couldn't source which journal systems Danish private clinics run, which leaves an integration gap to close first.

Language readiness

Partners will ask whether notes work in Norwegian, Danish and Finnish. The public site doesn't say. That's the first question to answer internally.

Partner economics

Pricing isn't public, so this page models clinics, not revenue. Reseller margins and member discounts need a decision before the first meeting.
